\paperabstract{%
  This demo is the \texttt{arxiv1col} venue talking about itself. It puts
  \texttt{acmart}'s \texttt{acmsmall} proportions on a US Letter sheet: every
  margin is the same fraction of the page it is in acmsmall, so the text block
  covers 0.811 of the width exactly as ACM sets it. Those fractions give a 6.9
  inch measure, 1.26 times acmsmall's, so the type scales with them -- Linux
  Libertine at twelve point on fourteen and a half point leading, which brings
  a line back to about seventy-nine characters against acmsmall's
  seventy-five. Pass \texttt{acmtrim} for acmsmall's own 6.75 by 10 inch page
  at ten point instead. Everything below shows what the settings do to real
  material.%
}

\section{Headings and body text}
\label{sec:headings}

A section head is Biolinum sans bold at the body size, set flush left, with
three quarters of a line above it and a quarter of a line below. That is
tighter than the \texttt{article} default, and it is what keeps an acmsmall
page dense without feeling crowded.

This second paragraph starts with a one em indent and no vertical glue, which
is how \texttt{acmart} separates paragraphs. Margin ratios alone do not make a
page readable: what governs a line is measure over type size, so widening the
measure to 6.9 inches without growing the type would push a line to ninety-five
characters. Scaling both keeps it near acmsmall's seventy-five.

\subsection{A subsection}
\label{sec:sub}

Subsection heads take the same font and the same spacing as section heads; only
the number tells them apart. Cross-references behave as usual:
Section~\ref{sec:headings} is above this one, and Table~\ref{tab:levels} lists
every level.

\subsubsection{A subsubsection}
The third level runs into the text, set in Biolinum italic and closed with a
period that paperkit adds for you. Write the title without the period; the
style supplies it.

\paragraph{A paragraph head} The fourth level runs in as well, in Libertine
italic and indented to the paragraph indent. It takes its own closing period
the same way.

\subsection{Math}

Inline math sits on the Libertine baseline through \texttt{newtxmath}, so
$\alpha, \beta, \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$ and $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ keep the colour of
the surrounding text rather than jumping to Computer Modern:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:softmax}
  \mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V)
    = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Measure and leading]
  \label{thm:measure}
  Scaling face, leading, and measure by a common factor leaves characters per
  line unchanged, so a page keeps its colour at any trim size.
\end{theorem}

  Left to the reader, who is looking at the evidence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tables and figures}

measure. Table~\ref{tab:levels} summarises the heading levels; the marks come

\subsection{Code and citations}

Listings use Inconsolata, the mono face acmsmall pairs with Libertine:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
make arxiv1col                # one-column preprint on US Letter
make OPTS=acmtrim arxiv1col   # acmsmall's own 6.75x10in page at 10pt
make OPTS=nopanel arxiv1col   # plain title block instead of the panel
\end{lstlisting}

Citations go through natbib in every venue mode, both parenthetical
\citep{vaswani2017attention} and textual \citet{kingma2015adam}.
